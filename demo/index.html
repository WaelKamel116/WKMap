Nasr City 3D Drive

Phase 0 demo prototype: a simulated drive westbound on El Nasr Road over two flyovers.

What it shows

  • A styled 3D city: real building footprints dressed in Nasr City facades (plaster in cream, sand and pink, red brick, glass), ground-floor shops, rooftop stair rooms, water tanks and satellite dishes. Lit windows at night.
  • Traffic lights that cycle, 60 km/h speed-limit signs, speed cameras, zebra crossings and overhead direction signs before each flyover fork.
  • Flyover level indicator: the app knows when you are up on the flyover or down on the ground road.
  • Lane guidance and junction view before each flyover fork, built from the lane counts in the map data.
  • Turn-by-turn banner with English and Arabic street names, arrival time, optional voice.

What is simulated or estimated

  • The car position is simulated along the route. No real GPS is used yet.
  • Building heights are estimated from footprint size. The source data has almost no heights for Nasr City.
  • Flyover deck height is modeled at 7 m with standard approach slopes. It is not surveyed.
  • Facades are generated from a small set of typical Nasr City styles. They are not photos of the actual buildings.
  • Only 7 traffic lights are mapped in OpenStreetMap here. The other 131 are estimated at junctions of two named main streets, and all of them cycle on a timer.
  • Speed-limit values, speed cameras and zebra crossings come from OpenStreetMap. The overhead direction signs are generated from the route's lane and road-name data.

Data

  • Streets, flyovers, lanes, parks, signals: OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).
  • About 2,450 buildings from OpenStreetMap plus about 3,270 added from Microsoft Global ML Building Footprints (ODbL).
  • Route geometry and maneuvers: OSRM.
